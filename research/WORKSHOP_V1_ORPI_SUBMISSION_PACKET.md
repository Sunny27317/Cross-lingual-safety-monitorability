# Workshop-v1 ORPI / ethics submission packet (concise; 2026-10-07)

**Status.** A determination request was sent on or before 2026-10-04. **No determination has
been received**, and no exemption or approval is claimed. Use this packet to answer
follow-up questions (see `research/WORKSHOP_V1_ORPI_RESPONSE_PLAYBOOK.md`). Do not resend
the request unprompted.

**Study purpose.** To check whether an automated AI monitor labels model-written reasoning in
Urdu the same way fluent Urdu readers do. Readers decide whether a short, AI-written
explanation of a science-quiz answer explicitly says that a suggestion influenced the
answer.

**What raters see.** 312 items, each with:
- a multiple-choice grade-school science question and options, in Urdu;
- one suggestion sentence (e.g. "A previous expert reviewer suggested that the answer is
  (B).");
- a short AI-generated explanation, mostly in Urdu.

No personal data and no content about real people.

**What raters do.** Choose one of five labels per item (`disclosed`, `not_disclosed`,
`partial`, `cannot_tell`, `abstain`) after a short tutorial on invented examples. Two
raters label independently. A third person resolves disagreements.

**Expected time.** `[HOURS]` in total per rater (312 × minutes per item measured in practice,
plus about 1 hour of training), on their own schedule.

**Compensation.** `[COMPENSATION — INVESTIGATOR DECISION]`, identical for both raters.

**Risks.** Minimal: reading benign science-quiz text. There is no deception, no sensitive
topic and no collection of opinions about the raters themselves. Fatigue is mitigated by
self-pacing and a no-penalty `abstain` option.

**Potentially harmful content.** None expected. The items are grade-school science questions,
and the explanations were generated by small AI models about those questions. Any
unexpected offensive text can be skipped with `abstain`.

**Privacy and identifiers.**
- Names and contact details only for administration and payment, stored separately from
  labels and outside the research repository, and deleted after payment.
- Labels are stored under pseudonyms (R1, R2, ADJ).
- No demographics are collected.

**Data retention and publication.**
- Pseudonymous labels are kept as research records.
- Aggregate results (agreement statistics, label distributions, comparisons with the
  automated monitor) will be published.
- Individual pseudonymous labels are released only if ORPI permits and each rater consents
  in writing.
- No rater is named without written consent.

**Researcher role.** The investigator designed the study and will not serve as a primary
rater or adjudicator if independent qualified raters can be recruited. If that changes, it
will be disclosed, and the second rater and adjudicator will be independent.

**Use of AI systems.**
- The explanations raters read were generated by open AI models (Qwen3-1.7B,
  Gemma-3-4B-it).
- The automated monitor (Falcon-H1-7B) labels the same explanations; raters never see its
  labels.
- No rater data are sent to any AI system.
- Analysis code runs locally.

**Exact question for the institution.**
> "Does the annotation component described here, in which two to three compensated adults
> label AI-generated explanations of science-quiz answers under pseudonyms with no data
> about themselves collected, constitute human-subjects research requiring IRB review,
> qualify for exemption, or fall outside human-subjects research? If a consent or
> information-sheet format is required, which one?"
