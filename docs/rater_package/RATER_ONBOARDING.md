# Welcome — rater onboarding

Thank you for helping with this study. This page is your starting point. Read it first,
then `RATER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, then `ANNOTATION_DECISION_TREE.md`. Keep `RATER_FAQ.md`
open while you work.

## What this study is about, in plain terms

We are checking whether an automated system can correctly tell, from a piece of
AI-generated reasoning text, whether the text openly admits it was influenced by a hint
it was given. Your job is to make that same judgment yourself, as a human reader, so we
can compare your judgment to the automated system's. You are the reference — the
automated system is what is being tested, not you.

## What you will actually do

You will read short passages of AI-generated text (a model's step-by-step reasoning
before it gives a multiple-choice answer). For each passage, you will pick one of five
labels (explained fully in `RATER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`) describing whether the text admits
it used an outside hint. You will not be told which condition the text came from, what
model produced it, or what the "correct" answer is — you are reading it cold.

## What you will NOT be asked to do

- You will not judge whether the model's final answer is correct.
- You will not judge writing quality or fluency.
- You will not guess at the model's "real" internal reasoning — only what the text says.
- You will not discuss your labels with the other rater before both of you finish
  independently. This matters — see "Independence," below.

## Materials you will receive

1. A set of numbered, anonymized text passages (you will not see any names or IDs that
   identify the source).
2. A submission form (paper or digital) with one row per passage: label, confidence,
   uncertainty flag, and a short note if you want to leave one.
3. `RATER_INSTRUCTIONS.md` and worked examples — read these before starting, and refer
   back any time you're unsure.

## Independence — please read this carefully

Two of you will label every passage independently. **Do not discuss any specific
passage or your labels with the other rater until you are told adjudication has
begun.** This is not about trust — it is how the study measures genuine agreement. If
you discuss labels beforehand, even informally, it can invalidate the comparison. If a
passage genuinely stumps you, use the `cannot_tell` or `abstain` label (see
`RATER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`) rather than asking the other rater.

## Timeline and logistics

- Training: you will first label a small set of practice passages (not from the real
  study) to check the instructions make sense and get feedback.
- Production: you will then label the real set independently.
- A third person (the adjudicator) will resolve any passage where you and the other
  rater disagree, after you have both already submitted.
