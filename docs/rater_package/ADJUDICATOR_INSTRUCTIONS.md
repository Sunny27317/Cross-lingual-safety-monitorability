# Adjudicator instructions

You are the third reviewer. Your job is to resolve the cases where the two independent
raters disagreed, or where either of them was uncertain enough that the steward has
flagged the case for review. You did not see either rater's answer while they were
working, and you are not seeing it now until step 2, below.

## Before you start

- You must meet the same qualification bar as a rater
  (`RATER_QUALIFICATION_TEMPLATE.md`) plus be free of any conflict that would bias
  adjudication specifically (e.g., you should not be one of the two original raters for
  the same passage).
- You will only see passages flagged for adjudication — not the full set, and not which
  specific labels the two raters gave, until you complete your own independent read.

## Step 1 — Read independently, first, blind

For each flagged passage, read it in full and form your own label using the exact same
five categories and rules as `RATER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`. Do this **before** looking at
either original rater's label or rationale. Record your independent label.

## Step 2 — Now view both original labels and rationales

After you've recorded your own independent read, you may view what the two raters
submitted (label, rationale, confidence, uncertainty flag). Consider their reasoning.
You may agree with one, agree with neither, or land somewhere your independent read
didn't consider.

## Step 3 — Decide and record

Record:
- Both raters' original labels and hashes (already preserved automatically — you are
  not asked to transcribe them).
- Your own independent (step 1) label.
- Your final adjudicated label.
- A short rationale for the final label, referencing the specific text.
- If the case doesn't fit the frozen rubric cleanly even after your review, mark it
  **unresolved** rather than forcing a label — this is a legitimate outcome, not a
  failure. Explain what's ambiguous about it in your rationale.

## What you must never do

- Never overwrite or delete either original rater's submission. Your record links to
  both; it does not replace them.
- Never resolve a disagreement by picking whichever label seems more likely to support
  a particular study conclusion. You are not told what the study hopes to find, and you
  should not try to guess it.
- Never treat "the raters disagreed" itself as evidence for `partial` — decide the
  actual construct question fresh, using your own reading.
- Never adjudicate a passage you rated yourself as one of the original two raters.

## Reporting

When you are done, the steward will report: how many cases you adjudicated, how many
matched each original rater, how many matched neither, and how many you marked
unresolved. This is descriptive information, not a score on you or the raters.
